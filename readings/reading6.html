Reading Response 6: Calm Technology

Monday, April 6th

Reading Link

I was struck when Weiser and Brown said that “[w]hat matters is not technology itself, but its relationship to us.” because personally, I think technology is cool and it’s exciting to see how quickly it advances, but when I look at my own relationship with technology, I have to admit that I’m often glued to my screen and technology is the one controlling me, not the other way around. The reading argues that as computing moves from personal devices to a world of ubiquitous, embedded computers where “many computers share each of us,” the central design challenge is to make calm technology that informs without overwhelming the user. Calm technology refers to designs that “engage both the center and the periphery of our attention, and move back and forth between the two.” I really liked the idea of the periphery, where we attune to things without actively attending to them. It explains why a “dangling string” (a piece of plastic spaghetti that twitches with network traffic) can feel so much more peaceful than a blinking notification, as it integrates naturally with our sensory processing. They say that when our periphery is functioning well and we are tuned into what is happening around us, as well as what has happened before and what will happen, that sense of “locatedness” is deeply human. Overall, this made me realize that we can build better, calm technology that empowers our periphery so that we can stay in control, even when we are surrounded by computers. However, if calm technology is so intuitive and humane, I wonder why most designers still prioritize the center over the periphery.
