Writing Exercise 1

Wednesday, April 8th

Question: What is a ritual, practice, or routine in your life that is important for your psychological wellbeing and/or fulfillment? Why?

Follow-Ups: How does this ritual or behavior contribute to your life? What aspect of it makes it valuable to you? What can it teach us about the nature or ingredients of human fulfillment more broadly?

One routine that is important for my psychological wellbeing and fulfillment is using Notion. I started using Notion in junior year of high school when one of my friends introduced it to me. I liked how the platform balanced structure with flexibility—the platform offers many built-in features and templates, but you can mix and match these elements like building blocks to make each page work for you. I’ve used Notion pages for travel boards, calendars, interview prep, reading lists, and more. Over time, I found what worked for me and what didn’t, and for the past 2 years, I’ve been consistently using two pages: my life organizer board, which I update every day to keep track of everything I need to get done, as well as my journal page, where I write about anything that’s bothering me or record meaningful things that happen in my life or the world that I want to remember. While my life organizer board changes daily and reflects everything I have going on in the moment, I use my journal page less frequently and more sporadically, and it’s a timeline of my thoughts spanning the past 3-4 years. Using Notion contributes to my life because it helps me let go. I always have so much going on all the time, and I don’t want to forget anything, which creates mental clutter. Organizing everything in Notion is a way to offload that burden because I trust that nothing will slip through the cracks. Seeing everything clearly laid out on my life organizer board gives me peace of mind and helps me feel like I’m in control of my life, and writing things down in my journal helps me let go and create space for me to live in the moment in new experiences. More broadly, this practice teaches us that human wellbeing and fulfillment often hinges on creating structure and a reliable external container for the chaos we sometimes feel internally. Whether it’s through a digital or physical tool, many of us use planners and journals because of our need to feel a sense of control over our commitments and goals, and it’s reassurance that our experiences and worries are somewhere safe.
