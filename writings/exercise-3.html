Writing Exercise 3

Wednesday, April 15th

Question: When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?

I cherish the slow or hard way of doing something when I want to learn how the process works and don't just care about the output. Sometimes, doing things manually the slow way is meditative. For example, I could bake a cake from scratch, or I could go to the grocery store and buy a pre-made cake or use a cake mix. I could use AI to generate a website for me in seconds, or I could mess around with the different components myself. I could walk a mile to Schnucks to get groceries, or I could take the shuttle there.

In all the examples when I do something manually, I feel like I'm growing because of the friction that you create when you sacrifice convenience. It means you get to appreciate the process, grow, and challenge yourself in a new way. Ultimately, I believe that human fulfillment is tied to getting better at something. The better technology gets at removing friction, things might become too convenient. We might be surrounded by perfect results, but we could feel less fulfilled and happy because we play a smaller part in creating those results.
